## These are adjacent products

Most comparisons on this site weigh two implementations of the same thing. This one does not.

authentik is an identity provider for single sign-on. Its value is that it speaks SAML, LDAP and
RADIUS as well as OIDC, which is what you need when the things logging in are appliances and bought
software you cannot change. FoxAuth is an OAuth 2.1 authorization server: it issues tokens to
applications you build, and it speaks nothing but OAuth and OpenID Connect.

If you are consolidating logins across an estate, authentik is the category of product you want and
FoxAuth is not. If you are securing your own applications and every one of them speaks OIDC, the
extra protocols are surface you will operate and never use.

## Where the overlap is real

Both issue OIDC tokens, and for that job the differences are narrow but specific. FoxAuth defaults
to OAuth 2.1 behaviour — PKCE mandatory for every client rather than only public ones, exact
redirect-URI matching, no implicit grant — because that is the whole product rather than one
protocol among four. And the banking-grade profiles are implemented here and not documented there.

Everywhere else on the table, the honest verdict goes the other way: a broader protocol surface, an
MIT-licensed core, and years more production deployment.
