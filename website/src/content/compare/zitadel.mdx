faq:
  - question: 'Does FoxAuth support SAML like Zitadel does?'
    answer: "FoxAuth does not support SAML, and none is planned. Besides OAuth 2.1 and OpenID Connect it serves SCIM 2.0, so a directory can provision users and groups into it, but it never speaks SAML. Zitadel documents SAML 2.0 and SCIM 2.0 alongside OIDC. If any application in your estate can only speak SAML, that settles it in Zitadel's favour and no other row on this page matters."
  - question: 'How do the licences differ?'
    answer: "Zitadel is AGPL-3.0 with Apache 2.0 and MIT exceptions for specific directories, and sells an enterprise contract whose commercial licence replaces the AGPL. FoxAuth is FSL-1.1-ALv2: you may read, modify, self-host and redistribute it and build a business around it, but not offer it as a competing hosted service. Each version converts to Apache 2.0 two years after it ships. AGPL's network-copyleft and FSL's competing-service restriction rule out different things, so which is the problem depends on what you are building."
  - question: 'Can I migrate from Zitadel to FoxAuth?'
    answer: "OIDC clients port over with an issuer and client id change. What does not port is anything relying on Zitadel's SAML support, its organisation model, or its management API; a directory that provisions over SCIM can be pointed at a FoxAuth project's `/scim/v2` instead. FoxAuth models tenancy as projects. A FoxAuth project is a tenant, not to be confused with a Zitadel project. Each one is its own issuer, addressed by a path beneath the server's URL, a subdomain of the instance or a hostname of its own, so a client integrates with one exactly as it would with any authorization server. Assess the migration by how much of your estate is OIDC-only, because that part is usually straightforward and the rest is not."
  - question: 'Which is more production-ready?'
    answer: "Zitadel is the more mature of the two, on deployment history and because its cloud has an SLA and enterprise contracts behind it. FoxAuth Cloud has a free plan but no SLA yet. FoxAuth reached its first stable release, 1.0.0, in October 2026, and has no deployment history to match. FoxAuth's advantage lies in specific protocol coverage and licence terms; it has none in maturity, and this page will not pretend otherwise."

## SAML usually decides it: Zitadel has it, FoxAuth has no plans for it {#the-row-that-usually-decides-it}

If anything in your estate speaks SAML and only SAML, this comparison is over: Zitadel documents
SAML 2.0, and FoxAuth does not implement it and has no plans to. Both serve SCIM 2.0 for
provisioning, so SCIM does not separate them. We left SAML out of scope by choice, so that gap will
not be filled, and it is the honest reason to choose Zitadel.

## FoxAuth is ahead in two narrow places: protocol profiles and licence {#where-foxauth-is-genuinely-ahead}

The first is the banking-grade profiles. DPoP, PAR, CIBA, mTLS, RAR and JARM are implemented and
flag-controlled on the instance you run. Zitadel's documentation does not describe them, so this
page says "not documented" instead of "absent": we will not make a claim about Zitadel that its own
documentation does not make.

The second is the licence, and only for some readers. AGPL-3.0 is an obstacle if you
distribute software or offer a network service built on it, and the enterprise contract that
replaces it is a commercial conversation. FSL-1.1-ALv2 has a narrower restriction: you may do
anything except offer FoxAuth as a competing hosted service. It also expires on a schedule, with each
version converting to Apache 2.0 two years after it ships.

Neither licence is permissive. If you need a permissive licence, neither of these is your answer.
