Assess the migration by how much of your estate is OIDC-only, because that part is usually straightforward and the rest is not."

## SAML usually decides it: Zitadel has it, FoxAuth has no plans for it {#the-row-that-usually-decides-it}

If anything in your estate speaks SAML and only SAML, this comparison is over: Zitadel documents
SAML 2.0, and FoxAuth does not implement it and has no plans to. Both serve SCIM 2.0 for
provisioning, so SCIM does not separate them. We left SAML out of scope by choice, so that gap will
not be filled, and it is the honest reason to choose Zitadel.

## FoxAuth is ahead in two narrow places: protocol profiles and licence {#where-foxauth-is-genuinely-ahead}

The first is the banking-grade profiles. DPoP, PAR, CIBA, mTLS, RAR and JARM are implemented and
flag-controlled on the instance you run. Zitadel's documentation does not describe them, so this
page says "not documented" instead of "absent": we will not make a claim about Zitadel that its own
documentation does not make.

The second is the licence, and only for some readers. AGPL-3.0 is an obstacle if you
distribute software or offer a network service built on it, and the enterprise contract that
replaces it is a commercial conversation. FSL-1.1-ALv2 has a narrower restriction: you may do
anything except offer FoxAuth as a competing hosted service. It also expires on a schedule, with each
version converting to Apache 2.0 two years after it ships.

Neither licence is permissive. If you need a permissive licence, neither of these is your answer.
